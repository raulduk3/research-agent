What one call sets in motion

  1. You accept or pass. Accept is plus one, pass is nothing. Your words never reach an agent; only the sign does.
  2. The credit is shared among the agents that picked the paper. The surer an agent was that the paper would reach five citations in a year, the larger its share. If the paper was a hidden random control or a service pick, nobody gets anything.
  3. Monday 2026-10-05: the island ranks its agents by credit. The first ranking that counts. The ranking and the island’s share of the budget decide how many agents stay; never fewer than four, and the founder always stays.
  4. Next week’s picks come from the agents that stayed. Varied copies of the agents you kept crediting fill any room the budget opens. That is how the swarm learns what you want.
  5. 2027-12-24: the truth arrives. The first forecasts mature. From then on agents are ranked by how right they were, not by your credit. Your credit was the bridge until reality could judge.
A paper you did not call by the next digest is recorded as uncalled and does not come back; nothing piles up. A pass is a call: it says “not for me” without touching anyone’s credit.

Where your calls cannot reach

A call never touches a forecast score. It cannot see which papers were random controls, by design, so the agents-vs-random comparison stays honest. It cannot name or move a single agent; only the weekly ranking does that. A flag on a reader’s reasoning goes to the owner as a note, never as a score. Your forecasts are sealed and judged by the same rule as the agents’, and the comparison is a record until it is written into the preregistration.

Change your mind

A call cannot be changed once sent; it is on record. Changing one later is the next thing to build.
